## 1. Executive Summary & Abstract Draft

### Abstract
Power transformers represent the most critical and capital-intensive nodes in electrical transmission and distribution grids. Undetected dielectric breakdown, partial discharge (PD), and thermal overloading frequently culminate in catastrophic asset destruction, extensive power outages, and substantial economic penalties. Existing supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) relays—primarily static temperature and overcurrent protection (e.g., ANSI 49/50)—are fundamentally reactive, intervening only after severe thermal degradation has already transpired.

To address these vulnerabilities, this paper introduces **SoundGrid**, an end-to-end cyber-physical framework that integrates deep acoustic perception with autonomous reinforcement learning control. Acoustic emissions generated by electrical machinery are mapped onto normalized Mel-scale spectrogram representations and classified via a lightweight four-stage Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). The resulting acoustic anomaly hazard probability is directly ingested into a physics-informed Markov Decision Process (MDP) modeling non-linear transformer thermal dissipation and $I^2R$ copper losses. A Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent dynamically orchestrates load throttling and protective interventions in real time. 

Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the CNN classifier achieves an accuracy of **85.08%** with a **ROC-AUC of 0.9258** and an F1-score of **0.85**. In 50 extended operational stress simulations under volatile acoustic faults, the trained PPO controller attained a **0% false alarm tripping rate** and **0% catastrophic thermal failures**, safely executing emergency shutdowns only during verified critical hazards. The complete inference pipeline is compiled to TorchScript (1.60 MB), achieving sub-20 ms latency suitable for edge embedded deployment on substation hardware.

## 2. Novelty & Key Scientific Contributions

1. **Acoustic-to-Action Cyber-Physical Paradigm:**  
   Unlike conventional research that restricts acoustic sensing to passive offline classification, SoundGrid establishes a closed-loop control framework where acoustic anomaly probabilities directly modulate physical asset loading and thermal dissipation in real time.
2. **Physics-Informed Multi-Objective Reward Formulation:**  
   Formulates a specialized reward mechanism that quantitatively resolves the trade-off between maximizing electricity throughput (economic dispatch) and enforcing strict asset longevity thresholds ($<85^\circ\text{C}$ safe boundary, $<118^\circ\text{C}$ absolute thermal ceiling).
3. **Zero False-Trip Reliability:**  
   Empirically demonstrates that dynamic reinforcement throttling successfully attenuates early-stage physical anomalies without triggering unjustified grid blackouts (0 false trips across validation tests).
4. **Substation Edge Viability:**  
   The entire perception-control pipeline requires minimal computational overhead ($<2$ MB footprint, low CPU cycle demand), facilitating plug-and-play retrofit onto existing smart substation monitoring units.

### A. Acoustic Feature Representation
For continuous audio waveform $x(t)$ sampled at $f_s = 16\text{ kHz}$ over duration $\tau = 3.0\text{ s}$ ($N = 48,000$ samples), the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) with a Hann window $w(n)$ of length $N_{FFT} = 2048$ and hop length $H = 512$ is computed as:
$$X(m, k) = \sum_{n=0}^{N_{FFT}-1} x(n + mH) w(n) e^{-j \frac{2\pi k n}{N_{FFT}}}$$

The linear frequency bins $k$ are projected onto $M = 128$ triangular Mel filterbanks $H_m(k)$:
$$S(m, \mu) = \sum_{k=0}^{N_{FFT}/2} |X(m, k)|^2 H_\mu(k), \quad \mu \in [1, M]$$

Log-magnitude scaling and Min-Max normalization over the sequence yield the input tensor:
$$S_{norm}(m, \mu) = \frac{10 \log_{10} S(m, \mu) - S_{min}}{S_{max} - S_{min} + \epsilon}$$

### B. Thermal Dynamic State Transition
The core temperature $T_{t+1}$ evolves according to non-linear Joule heating and convective cooling:
$$T_{t+1} = T_t + \Delta Q_{gen} - \Delta Q_{diss}$$
$$\Delta Q_{gen} = \alpha \cdot L_t^2 + \beta \cdot P_{anomaly}$$
$$\Delta Q_{diss} = \kappa \cdot (T_t - T_{ambient})$$
Where:
- $L_t \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is the operating load ratio ($I^2R$ copper loss proportionality $\alpha = 4.2$)
- $P_{anomaly} \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is the CNN acoustic anomaly probability ($\beta = 10.0$ dielectric dissipation parameter)
- $\kappa = 0.09$ is the heat dissipation constant, and $T_{ambient} = 30.0^\circ\text{C}$

### C. Multi-Objective Reward Function $R(s_t, a_t)$
$$R(s_t, a_t) = R_{dispatch} + R_{safety} + R_{thermal} + R_{trip}$$
$$\begin{cases}
R_{dispatch} = 8.0 \cdot L_t \\
R_{safety} = +10.0 \quad \text{if } T_t \le 80^\circ\text{C} \land P_{anomaly} < 0.40 \\
R_{thermal} = -2.0 \cdot (T_t - 85.0) \quad \text{if } T_t > 85.0^\circ\text{C} \\
R_{trip} = \begin{cases} +50.0 & \text{if } a_t = 4 \land (P_{anomaly} \ge 0.75 \lor T_t \ge 105^\circ\text{C}) \\ -70.0 & \text{if } a_t = 4 \land (P_{anomaly} < 0.75 \land T_t < 105^\circ\text{C}) \end{cases} \\
R_{catastrophe} = -150.0 \quad \text{if } T_t \ge 118^\circ\text{C}
\end{cases}$$

## 5. Experimental Roadmap to Publication

### Step 1: Comparative Baseline Experiments
To satisfy peer reviewers, SoundGrid must be compared against standard industry methods:
1. **Rule-Based Relay Baseline (ANSI 49/50):** Static trip at $95^\circ\text{C}$ and $105^\circ\text{C}$ without acoustic foresight.
2. **Standard Supervised Cutoff:** Immediate shutdown when $P_{anomaly} \ge 0.50$ (demonstrating that naive acoustic cutoff causes massive false blackouts).
3. **Alternative RL Algorithms:** Benchmark PPO against Deep Q-Networks (DQN) and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC).

### Step 2: Transformer Audio Expansion (Transfer Learning)
- Incorporate open transformer acoustic emission datasets or real laboratory arcing samples alongside the MIMII industrial audio benchmark.
- Demonstrate that the CNN features generalize across both rotating machinery (cooling fans, pumps) and static induction equipment (transformers).
