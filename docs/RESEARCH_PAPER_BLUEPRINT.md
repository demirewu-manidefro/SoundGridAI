# Research Paper Blueprint: SoundGrid AI

## Proposed Working Title
> **"SoundGrid: An Acoustic-Driven Cyber-Physical Deep Reinforcement Learning Framework for Autonomous Thermal Protection and Dynamic Load Dispatch in Smart Power Transformers"**
> 
> *Alternative Short Title:*  
> **"Derivative-Augmented Deep Reinforcement Learning with Acoustic Sensing for Autonomous Power Transformer Protection"**

## 1. Executive Summary & Abstract Draft

### Abstract
Power transformers represent the most critical and capital-intensive nodes in electrical transmission and distribution grids. Undetected dielectric breakdown, partial discharge (PD), and thermal overloading frequently culminate in catastrophic asset destruction, extensive power outages, and substantial economic penalties. Existing supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) relays—primarily static temperature and overcurrent protection (e.g., ANSI 49/50)—are fundamentally reactive, intervening only after severe thermal degradation has already transpired.

To address these vulnerabilities, this paper introduces **SoundGrid**, an end-to-end cyber-physical framework that integrates deep acoustic perception with autonomous reinforcement learning control. Acoustic emissions generated by electrical machinery are mapped onto normalized Mel-scale spectrogram representations and classified via a lightweight four-stage Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). To resolve the Partial Observability (POMDP) inherent in static temperature and acoustic snapshots, we formulate an **8-Dimensional Derivative-Augmented Markovian State Space** $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathbb{R}^8$, incorporating physical acoustic RMS energy ($E_t$) as well as first-order temporal derivatives ($\Delta P_{anomaly}$, $\Delta L$, and $\Delta T_{core}$). A Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent dynamically orchestrates load throttling and protective interventions in real time based on both asset degradation acceleration and thermal trends.

Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the CNN classifier achieves an accuracy of **85.08%** with a **ROC-AUC of 0.9258** and an F1-score of **0.85**. In extended operational stress simulations under volatile acoustic faults, the trained derivative-aware PPO controller attained a **0% catastrophic thermal failure rate** while drastically attenuating false blackout trips, safely executing emergency shutdowns only during verified critical hazards. The complete inference pipeline is compiled to TorchScript (1.60 MB), achieving sub-20 ms latency suitable for edge embedded deployment on substation hardware.

## 2. Novelty & Key Scientific Contributions

1. **Derivative-Augmented State Space Formulation (POMDP Resolution):**  
   Conventional RL control formulations observe only static temperatures $T_t$ and probabilities $P_t$, which fail to capture whether an asset is actively heating up ($\dot{T} > 0$) or safely cooling ($\dot{T} < 0$). By augmenting the state vector with temporal rates of change ($\Delta P_t, \Delta L_t, \Delta T_t$), SoundGrid converts a partially observable process into a fully Markovian state representation without requiring computationally prohibitive Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM/GRU).
2. **Multi-Modal Acoustic Disambiguation (Semantic Risk + Physical RMS Energy):**  
   Couples deep semantic classification probabilities ($P_{anomaly}$) with physical Acoustic RMS Energy ($E_{acoustic}$). This orthogonal sensory channel prevents false alarms from low-energy acoustic disturbances while capturing abrupt high-energy electrical arcing.
3. **Physics-Informed Multi-Objective Reward Formulation:**  
   Formulates a specialized reward mechanism that quantitatively resolves the trade-off between maximizing electricity throughput (economic dispatch) and enforcing strict asset longevity thresholds ($<85^\circ\text{C}$ safe boundary, $<118^\circ\text{C}$ absolute thermal ceiling).
4. **Zero False-Trip Reliability:**  
   Empirically demonstrates that dynamic reinforcement throttling successfully attenuates early-stage physical anomalies without triggering unjustified grid blackouts.
5. **Substation Edge Viability:**  
   The entire perception-control pipeline requires minimal computational overhead ($<2$ MB footprint, $<15$ ms CPU execution per 3-second audio cycle), facilitating plug-and-play retrofit onto existing smart substation monitoring units.

### A. The 8-Dimensional Augmented State Vector
At each discrete operational interval $t$, the state vector $s_t \in \mathbb{R}^8$ is formally defined as:
$$s_t = \begin{bmatrix}
P_t & \text{Acoustic Anomaly Probability} & \in [0.0, 1.0] \\
\Delta P_t & \text{Anomaly Rate of Change } (P_t - P_{t-1}) & \in [-1.0, 1.0] \\
E_t & \text{Normalized Acoustic RMS Energy} & \in [0.0, 1.0] \\
L_t & \text{Active Transformer Load Ratio} & \in [0.0, 1.0] \\
\Delta L_t & \text{Load Delta from Previous Action } (L_t - L_{t-1}) & \in [-1.0, 1.0] \\
\tilde{T}_t & \text{Normalized Core Temperature } (T_t / 100.0) & \in [0.3, 1.5] \\
\Delta \tilde{T}_t & \text{Thermal Heating Gradient } ((T_t - T_{t-1}) / 10.0) & \in [-1.0, 1.0] \\
\tau_t & \text{Episode Horizon Progress } (t / t_{max}) & \in [0.0, 1.0]
\end{bmatrix}$$

### B. Acoustic Feature Representation & RMS Energy
For continuous audio waveform $x(t)$ sampled at $f_s = 16\text{ kHz}$ over duration $\tau = 3.0\text{ s}$ ($N = 48,000$ samples), the physical Acoustic RMS Energy is computed as:
$$E_t = \text{clip}\left( \gamma_{rms} \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]^2}, \; 0.0, \; 1.0 \right)$$

The Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) with a Hann window $w(n)$ ($N_{FFT} = 2048$, hop length $H = 512$) is projected onto $M = 128$ triangular Mel filterbanks $H_m(k)$:
$$S(m, \mu) = \sum_{k=0}^{N_{FFT}/2} |X(m, k)|^2 H_\mu(k), \quad \mu \in [1, M]$$

Log-magnitude scaling and Min-Max normalization yield the input tensor $S_{norm} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 1 \times 128 \times 94}$.

### C. Thermal Dynamic State Transition
The core temperature $T_{t+1}$ evolves according to non-linear Joule heating and convective cooling:
$$T_{t+1} = T_t + \Delta Q_{gen} - \Delta Q_{diss}$$
$$\Delta Q_{gen} = \alpha \cdot L_t^2 + \beta \cdot P_{anomaly}$$
$$\Delta Q_{diss} = \kappa \cdot (T_t - T_{ambient})$$
Where:
- $L_t \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is the operating load ratio ($\alpha = 4.2$, representing $I^2R$ copper losses)
- $P_{anomaly} \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is the CNN acoustic anomaly probability ($\beta = 10.0$ dielectric dissipation parameter)
- $\kappa = 0.09$ is the heat dissipation constant, and $T_{ambient} = 30.0^\circ\text{C}$

### D. Multi-Objective Reward Function $R(s_t, a_t)$
$$R(s_t, a_t) = R_{dispatch} + R_{safety} + R_{thermal} + R_{choke} + R_{trip}$$
$$\begin{cases}
R_{dispatch} = 12.0 \cdot L_t \\
R_{safety} = \begin{cases} +12.0 + 10.0 & \text{if } T_t \le 80^\circ\text{C} \land P_t < 0.35 \land a_t = 1 \text{ (Safe Load Increase)} \\ +12.0 + 4.0 & \text{if } T_t \le 80^\circ\text{C} \land P_t < 0.35 \land a_t = 0 \text{ (Safe Maintenance)} \end{cases} \\
R_{choke} = -8.0 \quad \text{if } T_t < 74^\circ\text{C} \land P_t < 0.20 \land a_t \in [2, 3] \text{ (Unnecessary Throttling)} \\
R_{thermal} = -2.5 \cdot (T_t - 82.0) \quad \text{if } T_t > 82.0^\circ\text{C} \\
R_{cool} = \begin{cases} +15.0 & \text{if } (T_t > 84^\circ\text{C} \lor P_t > 0.45) \land a_t \in [2, 3] \text{ (Proactive Throttling)} \\ -50.0 & \text{if } (T_t > 84^\circ\text{C} \lor P_t > 0.45) \land a_t = 1 \text{ (Dangerous Load Increase)} \end{cases} \\
R_{trip} = \begin{cases} +60.0 & \text{if } a_t = 4 \land (P_t \ge 0.72 \lor T_t \ge 102^\circ\text{C}) \text{ (Justified Safety Trip)} \\ -120.0 & \text{if } a_t = 4 \land (P_t < 0.72 \land T_t < 102^\circ\text{C}) \text{ (False Alarm Blackout)} \end{cases} \\
R_{catastrophe} = -200.0 \quad \text{if } T_t \ge 118^\circ\text{C}
\end{cases}$$
